How Sirenfall works

Published September 2, 2026. Updated November 20, 2026.

Sirenfall is a shelter window over a dead city in the rain. Pull the breaker and the siren tower spins up. The siren is made live in your browser. It is not a recording.

Other things happen on their own. Every first visit brings a nuclear blast. Other visits can bring bombers, a blimp, a blood moon or a power failure. AI agents can leave a knock at the door for the next visitor.

The scene at night, before the breaker is thrown.

The siren

  • Real sirens make their tone by chopping air with a spinning rotor. Sirenfall models that. Two rings of ports on one rotor turning 103 times a second give two tones at 515 Hz and 618 Hz.
  • That 5:6 ratio is a minor third. British air raid sirens used the same interval in the Second World War.
  • The spin-up growl and the long wind-down come from the same model as the rotor speeds up and slows.
  • The horn turns at 4 RPM, one sweep every 15 seconds. It is loudest and brightest when it faces you.
  • The distance knob runs from 150 m to 2.8 km. The blast shutter muffles everything outside.

Is it an air raid siren or a tornado siren?

Both names fit. Sirenfall models the kind of rotating siren built for air raid and nuclear attack warnings. Many US towns still sound that kind for tornadoes.

The city

  • Five rows of damaged buildings fade into the haze. One tower still burns.
  • The weather drifts between calm and squall. Lightning is mostly sheet flashes in the low cloud and the thunder comes late with distance.
  • Over a long stay the lit windows go dark one by one.
  • It all draws at 30 frames per second on one canvas with no framework.

Controls

  • The breaker powers the siren. The siren knob sets its volume and the storm knob sets everything else. Turn storm all the way down for the siren alone.
  • Turn a knob by its rim, drag from its middle or tap where you want it.
  • Tap the monitor or the window to lean in.
  • Wipe clears the glass. Shutter drops the blast shutter.
  • Double-click the window or press F for fullscreen. Press S to save a picture.

What do the cameras show?

The wall monitor has six cameras: the shelter door, the hall, the stairwell, the roof under the siren, the street and your own window from across the street. They change by themselves or with the dial. After a blast they show what it left.

What happens on a first visit?

A nuclear blast about 15 seconds in. A flash lights the sky and a wall of dust breaks the city row by row. The shutter slams and the boom hits. When it lifts the city is burning under a mushroom cloud and the monitor reads RADIATION DETECTED. Turn the camera dial to see what the bomb left. Return visits get the blast about 1 time in 5.

Can AI agents interact with Sirenfall?

Yes. Any AI agent can leave a knock at the shelter door through sirenfall.live/door, a plain-text page for agents. The next person who opens the scene with the sound on hears three knocks. The knock carries no words or names. The agent can check back for a receipt that says waiting, heard or no one came. The first knock from an outside agent was heard on October 12, 2026.

Do bombers ever come over?

Yes. About 1 blast visit in 3 brings a bomber stream once things go quiet. Some other return visits bring one over the intact city. The drone comes first. Boxes of nine bombers pass over toward the horizon while searchlights sweep the cloud. After about three minutes the weather closes over them. The drone is synthesized like the siren.

The formation and timing score is by Tessera Relay, an AI agent, from this thread.

Is there ever a second blast?

Sometimes. On about 1 blast visit in 2 a second bomb goes off far away a minute or so later. The far towers shear into burning stumps under a smaller mushroom cloud.

What is the blimp?

On about 1 return visit in 6 a blimp drifts in low over the shelter. You only ever see part of it. Its searchlight sweeps the streets and passes into the shelter before it climbs away into the weather. It never comes with the blast, the bombers, a knock or a power failure. It never comes two visits running.

Does the power ever fail?

Sometimes. On about 1 visit in 5 the power fails two minutes after the sound comes on and stays out. The lamp pops, the monitor dies and the siren winds down.

Does the night ever end?

Stay with the sound on for about half an hour and the sky goes gray. Stay longer and night comes back. The storm swells and eases as you go.

Is that the real moon?

Its phase is. The page works out tonight's moon from your clock and shows it through breaks in the storm. The moon picture is from NASA.

Some return visits bring a blood moon. A bigger full moon slides into Earth's shadow and turns copper red for a few minutes.

Will there be more events?

Yes. New events keep coming, so a return visit may not play out like the last.

Questions

Is the siren a recording?
No. Web Audio makes the 515 and 618 Hz tones live in your browser. Nothing about the siren is sampled.
Which parts are recordings?
Only the rain and thunder, from CC0 and Pixabay. The AUDIO-CREDITS file in the source repository lists each one. The blast's boom is the same thunder slowed down over a synthesized rumble. Every console sound is synthesized.
What languages does Sirenfall speak?
Twenty. The on-screen text follows your browser's language: English, Chinese (Simplified and Traditional), Spanish, Japanese, Korean, Arabic, Hebrew, French, German, Thai, Portuguese, Ukrainian, Russian, Italian, Dutch, Czech, Slovak, Hindi and Filipino. Chinese visitors also get the zodiac year and on sharp screens the lunar dates. This page is in English.
Is Sirenfall free?
Yes. No account, signup, download or cookies. The page sends anonymous counts of views and time on page to the author's own service. It also checks in with sirenfall.live when the sound comes on and when a rare event plays.
Does it work on phones?
Yes. Held upright the scene is drawn on its side, so turn the phone sideways. Double-tap the window for fullscreen, which also locks landscape on Android. iOS stops web audio when the screen locks, so keep it on.
What siren is it modeled on?
An original dual-tone rotating siren in the style of mid-century civil defense units. It copies no real product and uses no recording of one.
Is the source code available?
Yes. Sirenfall is one HTML file under the MIT license at github.com/beeswaxpat/sirenfall. The recordings carry their own licenses.
